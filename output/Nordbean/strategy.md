# Nordbean updated product strategy — November 2026 to February 2027

Shift spend from discount-led subscription acquisition and low-margin gear retargeting into value-led subscriptions, single-origin demand in Austria and Switzerland, and the high-retention office plan. Keep gear available as a customer service, but stop paying to acquire gear orders where marketplace price competition is strongest. Budget split: P1 €8,000, P2 €7,000, P3 €0, P4 €5,000; total €20,000/month.

### Value-led subscription and gifting acquisition
| Product | Action | Replaces | Budget / month |
|---|---|---|---:|
| P1 | change | S1 | €8,000 |

**Plan**
1. **Google Ads — Query:** `kaffee abo` and `specialty coffee abo`; **Offer:** a P1 subscription with a free sample bag rather than a price-led discount; **Region:** Germany.
2. **Google Ads — Query:** `kaffee abo geschenk`; **Offer:** a giftable P1 subscription with a free brew guide; **Region:** Germany.
3. **Email lifecycle — Query:** `kaffee abo` acquisition cohort after first delivery; **Offer:** taste-profile guidance and a brew guide; **Region:** Germany.

**Expected result**: New subscribers per month increase while churn after the first delivery decreases.

**Justification**

- **Brand — Strategic priorities for 2026:** “Become the first choice for specialty coffee subscriptions in Germany among home enthusiasts.” This keeps P1 as the largest funded product and focuses acquisition on Germany.
- **Brand — Pricing principles:** “Discounts are limited to 20% and are never the main message of a campaign.” This requires replacement of S1’s 50%-off lead offer with added value.
- **Data — strategies file:** “| Churn after first delivery | 34% |” shows that the current first-delivery acquisition approach is losing too many new subscribers.
- **Data — products file:** “| Seasonality | Peak in November – December (gifts), +80% for "kaffee abo geschenk" |” supports prioritising the gift-intent subscription query at the start of this period.

### Stop paid gear acquisition; retain gear as a service
| Product | Action | Replaces | Budget / month |
|---|---|---|---:|
| P3 | stop | S3 | €0 |

**Plan**
1. **Google and Meta retargeting — Query:** `kaffeemühle test` and `handfilter set`; **Offer:** none—pause all paid gear retargeting; **Region:** DACH.
2. **Email — Query:** third-delivery P1 subscriber segment; **Offer:** none—end standalone gear-promotion emails; **Region:** DACH.
3. **On-site product pages — Query:** `handfilter set`; **Offer:** tested third-party gear at its listed price, with no price-match message; **Region:** DACH.

**Expected result**: Paid gear-acquisition cost falls to zero and gross-margin dilution from paid gear orders decreases.

**Justification**

- **Brand — Pricing principles:** “Brew gear is a service to coffee customers, not a profit centre. We do not try to win on gear prices.” This rules out continued paid acquisition in a price-comparison category while allowing the tested range to remain available.
- **Brand — What the brand does and does not do:** “We do: Sell third-party gear we have tested ourselves | We do not: Launch own-brand machines or capsules” This supports retaining only the curated third-party assortment.
- **Data — strategies file:** “| Gross margin | 18% |” shows limited room to absorb paid acquisition cost on P3.
- **Data — products file:** “| Price sensitivity | Very high: the same items are sold on marketplaces at lower prices |” shows why paid traffic is unlikely to be a defensible growth route for gear.
